# OpenEMR Trademark Usage Policy

## 1. Purpose

The OpenEMR Foundation, Inc. ("the Foundation") is the steward of the OpenEMR project and the owner of the OPENEMR trademarks. OpenEMR is free and open source software: anyone may use, modify, and redistribute the software under the terms of the GNU General Public License. The software license, however, does not grant rights to the OpenEMR name and logo. Trademarks exist so that users — including the clinics and health systems that depend on OpenEMR for patient care — can tell what is the genuine project, who stands behind a given product or service, and what the Foundation has or has not endorsed.

This policy explains how the OpenEMR marks may be used. Its goals are to protect users from confusion, to preserve the strength of the marks for the whole community, and to keep use of the name as easy as possible for the community members, contributors, and service providers whose work has sustained OpenEMR for more than two decades.

## 2. The Marks

This policy covers the following (together, "the Marks"):

- **OPENEMR** — the word mark, in any styling, in use in commerce since at least August 2002.
- **The OpenEMR logo** — the stylized "openEMR" design incorporating the electrocardiogram rhythm element.

The Foundation claims and asserts trademark rights in the Marks, including rights arising from more than twenty-four years of continuous use. Federal registration applications are on file with the United States Patent and Trademark Office. This policy applies regardless of the registration status of any particular mark.

## 3. Uses That Do Not Require Permission

The following uses are permitted without asking, provided they are truthful, do not suggest Foundation sponsorship or endorsement where none exists, and comply with the rest of this policy:

**a. Referring to the software.** Using "OpenEMR" to truthfully refer to the OpenEMR software, including in documentation, articles, books, courses, presentations, academic research, and comparisons.

**b. Describing your services.** Truthful statements that you provide services for the software, such as "hosting for OpenEMR," "OpenEMR support and customization services," "training for OpenEMR," or "we implement OpenEMR." Your own company and product branding must be the most prominent branding in such materials, and the statement must not imply that the Foundation endorses, certifies, or operates your business.

**c. Describing compatibility.** Statements such as "compatible with OpenEMR," "works with OpenEMR," "integrates with OpenEMR," or "built for OpenEMR" for products or services that truthfully do so.

**d. Distributing the unmodified software.** Distributing official, unmodified releases of OpenEMR under the OpenEMR name, with the Marks intact as released.

**e. Distributing the software with minor modifications.** Distributing OpenEMR with configuration changes, packaging changes, security patches, or modifications of the kind routinely required for deployment, under a truthful description (for example, "OpenEMR, packaged for [platform]"), so long as the result would be recognizable to the community as OpenEMR and the modifications are disclosed.

**f. Community use.** Use by user groups, meetups, community events, and non-commercial community projects to identify their connection to OpenEMR (for example, "the [City] OpenEMR User Group"), so long as the group does not claim to speak for the Foundation.

## 4. Uses That Require Written Permission

The following uses require prior written permission from the Foundation:

**a. Names and domains.** Use of "OpenEMR" (or a confusingly similar term) in a company name, product name, service name, trade name, domain name, social media handle, or app-store listing name.

**b. Substantially modified versions.** Distributing a substantially modified version of the software under the OpenEMR name. Forks and derived products are welcome under the software license, but they must be distributed under their own names, with a truthful statement of derivation permitted (for example, "derived from OpenEMR").

**c. Endorsement and affiliation.** Any use stating or implying that the Foundation sponsors, certifies, approves, or is affiliated with a person, product, service, or event, including designations such as "official," "certified," or "partner."

**d. Merchandise.** Use of the Marks on goods offered for sale (apparel, hardware, promotional items).

**e. The logo in commercial branding.** Use of the OpenEMR logo in commercial marketing, product interfaces, or materials beyond truthful reference to the software itself.

Requests should be sent to the Foundation at the address in Section 7. The Foundation grants permission through written license terms that include quality standards and may be revoked for non-compliance.

## 5. Rules That Apply to All Uses

- Use the Marks as adjectives identifying the software ("the OpenEMR software"), not as verbs or generic nouns for electronic health records generally.
- Do not alter, abbreviate, translate, or combine the Marks with other marks, words, or designs.
- Do not use the Marks in any way that is false, misleading, disparaging of the project or its community, or unlawful, or that suggests your offering is the official source of OpenEMR when it is not.
- When using the Marks in a commercial context under Section 3(b) or 3(c), include, where practical, an attribution such as: "OpenEMR is a trademark of OpenEMR Foundation, Inc. [Your company] is not affiliated with or endorsed by the OpenEMR Foundation."

## 6. The OpenEMR Ecosystem

A broad community of companies and individuals provides hosting, support, implementation, customization, and development services for OpenEMR. The Foundation welcomes this ecosystem; it is how free software reaches the clinics that need it. Uses of the Marks by ecosystem participants that conform to Section 3 are nominative, descriptive uses made consistent with this policy and with the Foundation's rights, and do not require individual licenses. The Foundation's tolerance of conforming descriptive uses is an exercise of this policy, not a waiver of its rights, and the Foundation reserves the right to object to any use that does not conform.

## 7. Permission Requests and Questions

Requests for permission, questions about this policy, and reports of misuse should be directed to:

The Foundation will respond to good-faith requests promptly and aims to say yes wherever user confusion is not at risk.

## 8. Reservation of Rights

The Foundation reserves all rights in the Marks. Failure to enforce this policy in any instance does not waive the Foundation's right to enforce it in that or any other instance. The Foundation may amend this policy by action of its Board of Directors; the current version maintained in the default branch of the Foundation's public governance repository at github.com/openemr/foundation-minutes (trademark/TRADEMARK_POLICY.md) governs, and the policy pages at open-emr.org and the project wiki refer to that version.
